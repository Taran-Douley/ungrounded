# Success criteria

Set 22 August 2026, before any promotion. Numbers chosen in advance so they can say no.

## By 30 September 2026

| | Modest | Target | Good |
|---|---|---|---|
| PyPI downloads | 150 | **400** | 1,500 |
| GitHub stars | 15 | **40** | 150 |
| Issues or discussions opened by strangers | 1 | **3** | 8 |
| **Teams who ran it against a production catalogue and told me the number** | 1 | **3** | 6 |

## The only row that counts

The last one. The others are proxies and two of them are barely that — PyPI downloads include mirrors, CI runs and bots, so a package with no human users routinely shows a hundred. Stars measure reach on one particular day, which is mostly outside my control.

**Hitting the first three rows and missing the fourth means this failed at what it was built for.** The point of shipping it was discovery: finding out what agent teams actually struggle with, using the measurement as the door-opener. A popular tool that produces no conversations with practitioners has not done that job, and I should say so rather than quietly counting the stars instead.

## If I miss

If by 30 September there are downloads and stars but nobody has sent back a production number, the conclusion is that the eval is not the wedge. Deciding that in advance, while I can still be honest about it, rather than after the fact.

The response then is not "promote harder". It is to go back to the calls and find out what people would actually pay attention to — because the finding will have been that this isn't it.

## What I'm deliberately not optimising

Launch timing, post titles, and anything else aimed at the top two rows. Those hours belong to fifteen targeted messages a day, which is the only activity that moves the bottom row.

30 September 2026. Record actuals against every row here, unedited, whatever they say.
